Breast Health Information

Breast Cancer & Tissue Conditions

Breast conditions include both benign and malignant tissue changes. The information below provides a simple overview of the histopathological categories included in this project. A tissue classification from an AI system should not be considered a medical diagnosis.

Benign Breast Conditions

🌸
Benign

Adenosis

What it is

Adenosis is a benign breast condition in which the glands or lobules become enlarged or more numerous. Some forms may appear unusual on imaging or under a microscope.

Common features

  • Usually benign
  • May produce a lump or thickened area
  • Often identified during breast examination or imaging

Diagnosis

Clinical examination, imaging such as mammography or ultrasound, and biopsy or histopathological examination when necessary.

🌷
Benign

Fibroadenoma

What it is

Fibroadenoma is a common benign breast tumor, particularly in younger people. It is not breast cancer.

Common features

  • Usually firm and smooth
  • May move somewhat under the skin
  • May be painless

Diagnosis

Clinical examination and appropriate imaging. Biopsy may be recommended when the diagnosis is uncertain.

🔬
Benign

Tubular Adenoma

What it is

Tubular adenoma is a rare benign breast tumor made primarily of closely packed tubular structures.

Precautions

A benign-looking lump still requires appropriate evaluation. Follow medical recommendations when an abnormality is identified.

Diagnosis

Imaging may identify the lesion, while histopathological examination can establish the diagnosis.

Malignant Breast Tumors

🧬
Malignant

Ductal Carcinoma

What it is

Ductal carcinoma begins in cells lining the milk ducts. Invasive ductal carcinoma has grown beyond the duct into surrounding breast tissue.

Possible signs

  • Breast lump or thickening
  • Change in breast size or shape
  • Nipple changes
  • Abnormal nipple discharge
  • Skin dimpling or other skin changes

Diagnosis

Clinical examination, mammography, ultrasound or MRI when indicated, followed by biopsy and microscopic examination.

🧬
Malignant

Lobular Carcinoma

What it is

Lobular carcinoma begins in the milk-producing lobules. Invasive lobular carcinoma occurs when cancer cells spread beyond the lobules into surrounding tissue.

Possible signs

  • Breast thickening or swelling
  • Change in breast shape
  • Sometimes no obvious lump
  • Other persistent breast changes

Diagnosis

Clinical examination, mammography, ultrasound or MRI when indicated, followed by biopsy and histopathological examination.

💗
Malignant

Mucinous Carcinoma

What it is

Mucinous carcinoma is a less common type of invasive breast cancer in which cancer cells are associated with substantial amounts of mucus called mucin.

Possible signs

  • Breast lump
  • Breast thickening
  • Changes detected through imaging

Diagnosis

Breast imaging, biopsy and histopathological examination. Biomarker testing may also help characterize the cancer.

🔬
Malignant

Papillary Carcinoma

What it is

Papillary carcinoma is characterized by distinctive papillary structures when examined microscopically.

Possible signs

  • Breast lump
  • Nipple changes
  • Nipple discharge
  • May sometimes be detected through imaging

Diagnosis

Mammography or ultrasound, biopsy and histopathological examination.

🌺
Benign / Borderline / Malignant

Phyllodes Tumor

What it is

Phyllodes tumors develop from the connective or stromal tissue of the breast. They can be benign, borderline, or malignant.

Possible signs

  • Rapidly enlarging breast lump
  • Breast enlargement or asymmetry
  • Skin stretching over a large mass

Diagnosis

Clinical examination, ultrasound or mammography, tissue sampling and histopathological examination.
